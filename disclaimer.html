RTY (Rolled Throughput Yield)

Overview

Last updated: October 8, 2026

RTY is a calculation tool. It multiplies the first-pass yields of the steps you enter to give a rolled throughput yield, works out a normalised yield and a Pareto of yield loss, and lets you test what-if changes. This page explains the limits of what that means in practice.

Results depend entirely on your data

Every figure the app produces is only as accurate as the numbers you provide. If a step's units in, units out, pass rate or Cpk is entered incorrectly, the RTY, the Pareto and everything built on them will be wrong too, even though the arithmetic is correct. Always review what you have entered before relying on the results.

Measure every step the same way

RTY only means something when every step is counted on the same basis: first-pass good units against units started at that step. Mixing figures that include rework with figures that do not will make a step look better than it is, and the RTY will be overstated.

A Cpk is not a measured pass rate

When you enter a Cpk, the app converts it to a pass rate by assuming a centred process with a normal distribution and no 1.5 sigma shift. Real processes drift, are not always normal, and are rarely perfectly centred, so the pass rate from a Cpk is usually better than what the process delivers over the long term. Real counts of units in and out are the more honest figures.

The Funnel and What-if are illustrations

The Funnel is a random simulation built from your pass rates, and no two runs are the same. The What-if tab and its target solver are arithmetic, not forecasts. They do not know whether an improvement is achievable, what it would cost, or whether it would affect other steps. Use them to explore and to prioritise, not to predict.

Build trust before relying on it

Before using RTY for a decision that matters, run a process through it where you already know the answer from another method or tool. Comparing what RTY produces against that known result is the quickest way to build genuine confidence in it, the same way you would validate any new tool before putting it to real use.

Not a substitute for professional judgment

RTY does not replace calibrated measurement equipment, a formal quality management system, statistical training, or your organization's own quality procedures. It is a calculation aid intended to support someone who already understands what these figures mean and how they apply to their specific process.
